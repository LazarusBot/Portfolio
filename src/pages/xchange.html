Context

XChange is the app crews use to get online at sea. Every minute of connection is paid for, from up to three places: an allowance from the company, the crew member's own personal credit, or an eWallet for links like Starlink Direct. Each can be on, empty, or simply unavailable on the current link.

The problem

The old dashboard updated in jumps. Numbers changed without warning and the screen flipped from one state to another, so crews couldn't tell at a glance whether they were online, or which credit was being spent. We called it status anxiety: on a paid satellite link, not knowing is the worst state to be in.

Where it started

The first drafts gave each role its own home, for the crew, the captain and the admin: a big plug-shaped button, a price per MiB and the list of satellite links. Everything was there, but in network terms, and the state of the connection was one small dot next to the name.

About thirty tries, then tiles

The connection panel went through around thirty versions: credits as lines, as sliders, with a voucher field, with warnings for a session already open on another device, the links shown as their logos. The first version settled on tiles: the three credits side by side, each one you switch on, under one big button.

One screen, every state

Every state the link can be in got its own screen, and instead of jumping, the tiles and the button morph from one to the next, so the change itself tells you what happened.

Edge cases first

Each state was written as a user story with the team before it was drawn: eleven stories for the Crew Welfare proof of concept, most of them about what happens when something is missing.

  • Keep credits and quota visible when opening a session is impossible
  • Choose the wallet when the session opens
  • No wallet assigned
  • Hide everything eWallet when the feature isn't activated
  • Every combination at credit selection
  • A pop-up for link errors, success and waiting

Seeing where the credit goes

MyUsage answers the question that comes right after connecting: what did that cost? The three credits sit like cards in a wallet, every session and top-up is listed with its source, and filters narrow the history by credit, device or type.

And outside the app: a Live Activity counts down to the next time window on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island, and the news sit in a widget.

On desktop

The same panel and components ran in the browser, in light and in dark.

Components

The first set of components: buttons for each state, the credit tiles, the cards and the history rows. Version 2 grew it into the full design system.

The final design: the balance first

Version 2 kept the states and dropped the noise. The credit in use is the hero, with its trend drawn behind it, the link it runs on, and one button. Swipe to switch between the company, personal and eWallet credit. Under it, the last seven days and a one-tap credit request.

A bar for the time window

The anxiety was mostly about what's left. So the balance got a bar, and I tried it in every place it could live before keeping it under the number: at the top, under the balance with what remains in words, as a gauge in a panel. When it runs out, the button turns into the next step.

The rest of the app

Every session and top-up in one list, the documents a crew member needs on board, a profile, a sheet that explains what changed in an update, and a menu that stays out of the way.

Also on the web

The same credits, chart and seven-day overview run in the browser, as a dashboard, in light and in dark.

The system

A design system of 500+ components covers mobile and web: IBM Plex Sans with a clear type scale, a blue brand palette with one colour per meaning (success, error, accent, dark), a set of icons drawn for the app, and a button for every state of the link, from Connect to Sealink to Refill my wallet. I built it in Figma and kept it in step with the Swift and SwiftUI code, so what was designed is what shipped.

Result

The app shipped on iOS and reached more than 200,000 users. Designing it and building it myself meant the states, the motion between them and the edge cases made it into the app as drawn.
